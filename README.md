# Exploring Lysosomal Enzymes Using NCBI and R

## NCBI Record 

**Gene Symbol:** SMPD1

**Gene Name:** Sphingomyelin phosphodiesterase 1

**Enzyme Name:** Acid sphingomyelinase

**NCBI Protein Accession:** NP_000534.3

**NCBI Protein Record Link:** https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/protein/NP_000534.3

**What It Breaks Down:** Sphingomyelin into ceramide and phosphocholine.

**Normal Function:** Breaks down sphingomyelin in lysosomes, supporting normal lipid metabolism and cell membrane maintenance.

## Questions
**1. What does your enzyme normally break down inside lysosomes?**
SMPD1 produces acid sphingomyelinase, an enzyme that breaks down sphingomyelin, a type of lipid found in cell membranes, into ceramide and phosphocholine inside lysosomes. This process helps the cell recycle membrane components.

**2. What is the total length of your protein, and which amino acids are most common?**
Based on the amino acid composition graph, leucine (L) is the most abundant amino acid, with approximately 81 residues, followed by proline (P) with 63 and alanine (A) with 55. The protein is approximately 629 amino acids long, based on the position range in the motif graph.

**3. How many potential N-glycosylation motifs did you find? Where are they located?**
Six potential N-glycosylation motifs were found in the SMPD1 protein. Based on the figure, they are located at approximately amino acid positions 90, 176, 335, 397, 505, and 522. These sites follow the N-X-S/T sequence pattern, where X cannot be proline.

**4. Why can glycosylation matter for lysosomal proteins? Does finding a motif prove the protein is glycosylated?**
Glycosylation can help lysosomal proteins fold properly, remain stable, and function correctly. However, finding a potential motif does not prove that glycosylation occurs at that site. Experimental evidence is needed to confirm it.

**5. What could happen in the cell if your enzyme did not work properly?**
If SMPD1 does not work properly, sphingomyelin can accumulate inside lysosomes, disrupting normal lipid breakdown and cell function. SMPD1 defects can cause Niemann-Pick disease types A and B, which may affect organs such as the liver, spleen, and nervous system.
